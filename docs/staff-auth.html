Measurements — build to these exactly

The prototype banner is on every authentication screen, and it is not a footnote

1.5px dashed unrecorded, 5px left border, the hatch, 14px 18px. It says plainly: there is no authentication in this build. Any details sign you in, no password is checked, and nothing is protected. This is the largest "control that does nothing" in the product and the only honest way to ship it is to say so at the point of use. A sign-in form that silently accepts anything is worse than no sign-in form, because it implies a check that is not happening.

1 · Sign in

1fr 1fr, minimum 620px. Left is brand purple with the product's own claim on it; right is the form, 400px max width. Email, password, and which home you are signing in to — the site is chosen here rather than after, because it decides the timezone every record you write will carry, and choosing it later means the first thing you read was in the wrong zone. No "remember me": there is no session to remember.

2 · Accepting an invitation

560px, centred. The invitation states what the manager set — role, home, and the access level that comes with it — before the password fields, because somebody accepting an invitation should see what they are accepting. Password rules render as a live checklist, each ticking as it is met. No rule is hidden until it fails. The invitation expires, and the screen says when: an invitation with no expiry is a permanent open door.

3 · A staff member's own dashboard

This is "what is mine", not "what is late here". The site Dashboard is the home's; this is one person's shift. Three tiles: the round they are on now (brand purple, largest figure), what they have flagged that is still waiting, and residents on their list nobody has written up. The third takes the hatch — it is the only tile counting an absence. Beneath, two panels: what they have recorded today, and what they can do. No counts of their own work, no rate, no comparison with anybody. Phase 14's constraint applies with more force here, not less — a screen showing somebody their own productivity figures is a performance record whoever built it says it is not.

4 · What I can do

Sixteen modules, four levels, read-only. The statement that nothing is enforced renders before the first row, as it does on the manager's matrix. Beneath it, the "not held here" block in its inert treatment: supervision, appraisal, training and induction are not in this product, and a member of staff looking for their own training record should be told plainly rather than left to conclude it is empty.

5 · Signing out — the one screen with a real consequence

Everything this build writes is held in memory. Signing out discards it, and this is the only place in the product where an action destroys work rather than failing to save it. So the confirmation names what would be lost, item by item, with counts — the same shape as the consent withdrawal's downstream effects, and for the same reason: a sentence saying "unsaved changes will be lost" cannot be checked against what is actually there. Where nothing has been written this session, the block does not render and the screen is a plain confirmation.
