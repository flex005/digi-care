Measurements — build to these exactly

Four rows, four chart types

Row 1: four stat cards, repeat(4,1fr), gap 16px — figure 34px/800 with its denominator beside it at 12px/600, and a seven-day sparkline at 34px tall beneath. The sparkline is the last seven days of that same figure; the dot marks today. Row 2: 1.85fr 1fr — an area chart of doses recorded against due over seven days, and a donut of today's rounds. Row 3: 1fr 1fr — horizontal bars of what the record holds by module, and one ring per round today. Row 4: the dense table, full width. Outer radius 20px throughout, gap 16px.

The hatch is an SVG pattern, so charts carry it

<pattern id="hatch"> at 45°, used as a fill on the area band, as a stroke on the donut's no-record arc, and as the track behind each incomplete ring. Missing evidence is never a lighter shade of the recorded colour — it is the same hatch as every screen in the build, which is what keeps the charts readable in greyscale and to a colour-blind reader.

Every chart shows recorded against expected

Not a percentage against a target. The area chart's shaded band is the gap between what was recorded and what was due; the donut's segments are recorded, no-record, due-now and not-due-yet; the bars are recorded and expected-and-missing. A chart of a percentage would hide its own denominator, which is Rule 4 applied to a shape rather than a figure.

Sparklines carry direction, never a bare arrow

Seven points, today marked with a dot. No "↑5%" — a trend arrow with no denominator is the shape this build spent fifteen phases removing, and the line itself carries the direction honestly.
