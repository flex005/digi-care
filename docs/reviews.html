Completed filter — the one genuinely new treatment

When the completed filter is active the screen's fact changes, and so does what leads. A queue of completed records has no gap to lead on, so **the lead becomes an Aggregate rather than a finding**: coverage over the range, with its denominator, and how much of it was done late. Coverage block 20px 24px, figure 38px/800, bar 8px split into done and late segments, key beneath at 11.5px. Rows render the completed review as a settled record — plain text, no chip, author and both dates always visible. **Quiet is not hidden**: this is the evidence a review happened, and until now it existed in the data and appeared on no screen at all. A review completed late still says so, in one line at 11px/700 critical ink beneath the settled text. Lateness is derived, not stored, and it survives being completed.

Whole-plan review session

Every domain listed with its own state — the same list the care plan tab renders, read-only here. **The session can be completed while domains are gaps**, and the record stores which ones were. Same pattern as the handover signature: refusing would mean the meeting happened and the system holds no evidence of it, and permitting it silently would let "care plan reviewed" sit over three domains nobody has written. The "what this will record" block is hatched, 15px 19px, 5px left border, and names each outstanding domain individually rather than counting them. Footer: the state line says what completing records, including the gaps it will carry.

What must not happen

Do not add a whole-plan review to the profile as a tab. Each module already answers for its own records, and a second place to see the same thing is where the two start disagreeing. Do not queue the social worker review. That is the local authority's review of a placement, not the home's review of a record — different owner, and a queue that mixes them makes the home look accountable for somebody else's diary.
